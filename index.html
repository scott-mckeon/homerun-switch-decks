They all resell the same grid

every one just resells access to
The grid · Sydney's poles and wires (a regional monopoly)
Ausgrid

Same electrons, same wires, whoever you pay. In Sydney, every retailer is on-selling the exact same access to Ausgrid's grid. The retailer is just billing and marketing on top.

So what are they competing on? Marketing.

$125m
what AGL spent on advertising and marketing in a single year
  • They sponsor football clubs, run TV campaigns, and buy your attention
  • Fossil fuel companies spend $14m to $18m a year just sponsoring Australian sport
  • None of that spend makes your actual electricity any different or cheaper

Sources: AGL's own half-year accounts (H1 FY25) show $63m on campaigns and advertising in six months, roughly $125m a year; Guardian Australia on sport sponsorship.

This has a name

A Confusopoly

A market where companies make their pricing deliberately confusing, so you can't compare, which lets them keep prices high. Energy, telco and insurance are the textbook cases.

Why they can afford to give you $200

What they give you
$200
A welcome offer, once. Usually a credit on your first bill.
then
What it quietly costs you
~$221 / year
The loyalty tax. What long-standing customers pay above the best offers, every year they stay.

They give it back once, because they know you'll stay for years.

The offer is bait. But it's your potential benefit, if you regularly switch.

Source: ACCC, households on the same plan 3+ years pay around $221 a year more.

The Reference Price doesn't rescue you

$1,965
the NSW reference price a year: Ausgrid, a 3,900 kWh benchmark, set by the AER
  • 1 It assumes 3,900 kWh a year. That's not your usage. Yours could be half that, or double.
  • 2 It's one fixed annual number. It can't reflect how you actually live.
  • 3 Retailers game it. "18% off the reference price" is measured against a benchmark that isn't you.

Source: AER, Default Market Offer 2025-26, Ausgrid residential without controlled load.

The good news: you only need two numbers

Usage
Supply
Daily supply charge. A fixed cost every day just to be connected, even at zero usage. Like line rental.
Usage rate. What you pay per unit you actually use. The meter ticking.

Time of use and controlled load also exist. If you have those, it's advanced mode.

The prompt, ready to copy

I'm comparing two energy plans for my own usage. I'll paste my current
plan's rates and usage from my last bill, and a new plan's rates.

Please:
1. Compare them using MY usage.
2. Tell me which is cheaper per year for my usage, and by how much.
3. Split it: ongoing saving on rates vs any one-off welcome credit.
4. Subtract the one-off welcome offer so I see the true ongoing position.
5. Show your working, then re-check every number against what I pasted
   and flag anything you're unsure about.
Do not guess missing numbers. Ask me if anything isn't clear.
